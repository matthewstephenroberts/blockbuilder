# License intent & educational purpose

## Why MIT?

BlockBuilder is released under the **MIT License** because:
- **Maximum accessibility** — educators, students, and hobbyists should be able to use, study, and
  modify this tool without restriction.
- **Legal simplicity** — MIT is widely understood and trusted by the open source community.
- **Community collaboration** — contributions (new catalog parts, cell types, geometry
  improvements) make the tool better for everyone using it.

## Why this statement?

MIT places no legal restriction on commercial use. This document is about **intent and values**,
not additional legal terms — it explains what BlockBuilder is *for*, even though the license
permits other uses.

## Core purpose

**BlockBuilder exists to help people design, understand, and 3D-print their own
Technic-compatible parts** — for education (learning CAD, CSG, and mechanical design concepts),
for makers extending an existing collection with parts that aren't sold individually, and for
prototyping mechanisms before committing to a purchase.

It's a geometry tool: given a grid of parts you paint, it computes real CSG boolean operations to
produce a single watertight mesh and exports it as STL. It does not include, embed, or reproduce
any copyrighted models, printed decorations, minifigure designs, or official set files — every
part it builds is generated from parametric dimensions.

## Who this is for

### Educators and students
- Use BlockBuilder freely for coursework, workshops, or personal learning.
- Modify it, extend the part catalog, or use it to teach CAD/CSG/mechanical-design concepts.
- You'll never be charged for core features.

### Makers and hobbyists
- Design replacement or extension parts for your own projects.
- Build on this work and share your improvements.

### Commercial users
You're welcome to use this in your own product. We ask that you:
1. **Credit the project** — mention BlockBuilder and acknowledge the MIT license in your
   documentation.
2. **Don't claim original authorship** or strip attributions from derivative works.
3. **Consider contributing back** — new catalog parts, bug fixes, or documentation improvements
   benefit everyone using the tool.

### Researchers and non-profits
- Build on this work freely. MIT already permits it — this is just to say clearly: go ahead.

## What we'd discourage (not a legal restriction)

- Reselling BlockBuilder unchanged as a finished product without attribution or added value.
- Removing credits or open-source notices from derivative works.
- Marketing generated parts as official products of any brand.

MIT doesn't legally prevent these things, and that trade-off is intentional — we'd rather keep the
license simple and trust the community to share these values than add legal complexity that makes
the project harder to use for its actual audience: people who want to design and print their own
parts.
